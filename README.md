# Teaching Fractions — Free Interactive Fraction Visualizer for Kids

**Learn fractions by cutting, moving, comparing, and combining colorful fraction blocks.** Teaching Fractions is a free, interactive math learning tool for parents, teachers, homeschoolers, and children. It turns abstract fraction problems into something students can see and explore, with answers hidden until they are ready to check their work.

## Why I made this

I originally built **Teaching Fractions** to help teach my son, **Zaqxen Rei**. I wanted a way to demonstrate fractions that went beyond looking at pictures or checking whether an answer was right.

There are other fraction activities online, but I was looking for a combination of features I could use while teaching: **cut a whole into pieces, move those pieces, make equivalent fractions, compare amounts, join them, and explain each calculation step by step**. Most importantly, I wanted my son to *try the problem first* instead of seeing the answer immediately.

I'm sharing this project for free so **other parents and teachers can use it with their children and students**—at home, in a classroom, during tutoring, or while homeschooling. It is a teaching aid, not a test: explore, make mistakes, ask questions, and discover how fractions work.

## Start here: a 5-minute fraction lesson

1. **Open the [interactive fraction lab](https://zaqxen.github.io/teaching-fractions/).** You will see a **Cutting board**, **A — Top fraction**, **B — Bottom fraction**, and an **Addition** area.
2. **Make halves.** On a computer, **right-click** the whole block on the Cutting board and select **Cut into 2**. On a touchscreen, **press and hold** the block for the same menu.
3. **Make quarters.** Right-click one half and cut it into 2 again. Ask: *How many quarters fit in a half?*
4. **Compare.** Drag a `1/2` block from the Cutting board into **A**, then place two `1/4` blocks in **B**. Dragging from the Cutting board **copies** the pieces, so the original source remains available.
5. **Ask the child to predict.** Do the top and bottom bars end at the same place? What comparison symbol belongs between A and B?
6. **Reveal the explanation.** Click **Show Comparison Solution** only after the child has made a prediction. You can also use a row's **Show Answer** button for its fraction calculation.

This visual example demonstrates **equivalent fractions:** `1/2 = 2/4`.

## How to use each part of the tool

### 1. Cutting board: build fractions

- **Cut a block:** Right-click a block (or press and hold it on touchscreens), then choose **Cut into 2, 3, 4, 6, or 8**, where supported by the piece-size limits.
- **Enter a fraction without cutting:** Open the context menu on a Cutting board block and choose **Enter fraction…**. Type the **numerator** (top number) and **denominator** (bottom number). The visual blocks are created for you.
- **See the part and the remainder:** Enter `3/4` to see a `3/4` block alongside the remaining `1/4`. The entered amount glows briefly for emphasis.
- **Explore fractions greater than one:** Enter `5/2` to see two full wholes and an additional half, with whole boundaries shown separately.
- **Keep your source pieces:** Drag pieces from the Cutting board to another row; the original pieces stay available for the next demonstration.

**Teaching prompt:** “If I take three of four equal parts, how much is left to make one whole?”

### 2. Comparison A and B: see which fraction is larger

- Move or copy blocks into **A — Top fraction** and **B — Bottom fraction**.
- Both rows use the **same visual scale**, so children can compare where the colored sections end.
- The middle symbol shows **`<`**, **`>`**, or **`=`**, depending on the fractions.
- Click **Show Comparison Solution** for the full explanation rather than giving away the calculation before the student is ready.
- Click the **comparison symbol itself** to copy the pieces from **both A and B into Addition**, whether the fractions are equal or not.
- Use **Clear A** or **Clear B** to set a new example.

**Teaching prompt:** “Which is larger: `1/2` or `1/3`? Does a larger bottom number always mean a larger piece?”

### 3. Addition: join pieces while keeping their original cuts

- Drag pieces into **Addition**, or click the comparison symbol to bring in copies from A and B.
- **Drop one fraction block on another** to combine them. A popup explains the addition, including a common denominator when needed.
- The Addition display shows **Whole 1**, **Whole 2**, and more wholes as necessary. Whole boundaries remain visible even when a piece extends past one whole.
- The visual pieces keep their **original fraction identity** as they cross a whole boundary. This helps children see how much of a piece continues into the next whole.
- The running total is **not shown automatically**. Click **Show Answer** to see the full calculation and solution.
- Use **Merge blocks**, **Clear Addition**, or **Undo** while experimenting.

**Teaching prompt:** “What happens when we put `3/4` and another `3/4` together? Can we fill one whole and still have some left?”

### 4. Simplifying fractions and mixed numbers

- **Double-click a block** or open its right-click menu and choose **Simplify** to see a worked solution.
- For a fraction such as `6/8`, the popup shows how dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor gives `3/4` without changing its value.
- In Addition, an improper fraction such as **`11/8`** can be taught in two visual stages:
  1. Double-click `11/8` to separate it into **`8/8 + 3/8`**, displayed across Whole 1 and Whole 2.
  2. Double-click the **`8/8`** block to simplify it to **`1`**, leaving **`1 + 3/8`**, or **`1 3/8`** as a mixed number.
- The student can see **what changed mathematically** and **what stayed the same in the visual size**.

**Teaching prompt:** “How many complete wholes are inside `11/8`? What fraction is left over?”

### 5. Helpful teaching controls

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Undo** | Reverts the last fraction action so you can try again. |
| **Start with 1 whole** | Resets the activity to a single whole block. |
| **Load 1/2 vs 2/4** | Opens a ready-made equivalent-fractions example. |
| **Try 1/2 vs 3/6** | Opens another equivalent-fractions example. |
| **Show Answer / Show Comparison Solution** | Reveals calculations and explanations only when requested. |
| **Teacher guide** | Opens built-in instructions for using the controls. |
| **Draw a temporary arrow** | On a computer, left-click and drag on **empty page space** to point something out. The arrow stays while you hold the mouse button and disappears a few seconds after release. Dragging a block still moves the block instead. |

## Ready-to-use activities for parents and teachers

| Activity | What to build | What the child discovers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Part of a whole** | Enter `3/4`; examine the remaining `1/4`. | Numerator, denominator, and the missing part of a whole. |
| **Equivalent fractions** | Compare `1/2` with `2/4`, or `1/2` with `3/6`. | Different fraction names can represent the same amount. |
| **Which is greater?** | Compare `1/2` and `1/3`. | Fraction size depends on the size of each equal part. |
| **Add fractions visually** | Add `1/4 + 1/4`, then try `1/4 + 1/8`. | Adding like fractions and finding common denominators. |
| **Go past one whole** | Add `3/4 + 3/4`. | Fractions can total more than one whole. |
| **Improper to mixed number** | Build `11/8` in Addition and double-click it. | `11/8 = 8/8 + 3/8 = 1 3/8`. |
| **Simplify without shrinking** | Simplify `6/8` to `3/4`. | Simplifying changes the numbers, not the amount. |

### A simple teaching approach

**Predict → Build → Compare → Explain → Reveal.** Ask your child or student to predict the result, let them move the pieces, talk through what they observe, and open the solution popup *last*. You can use the temporary arrow to draw attention to a bar or boundary without selecting text on the page.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Teaching Fractions free for students, parents, tutors, and teachers?**  
Yes. It is a free, open-source fraction learning tool. You can use it at home, at school, or for tutoring.

**Do children need an account or internet connection?**  
No account is needed. Open the live website to use it online, or save `index.html` and open the file locally to use it without an internet connection.

**Does it work on phones or tablets?**  
The interface is responsive and supports touchscreen interactions. Press and hold a fraction block to open the cut/simplify menu. For extended demonstrations, a larger screen may be easier to use.

**Does it show answers right away?**  
The activity is designed to delay the numerical solution until a **Show Answer** or solution control is selected. This helps the child reason with the visual blocks first. The visible comparison symbol still provides immediate `<`, `>`, or `=` feedback.

**What topics can it help teach?**  
Parts of a whole, numerators and denominators, equivalent fractions, comparing fractions, adding fractions, common denominators, simplifying fractions, improper fractions, and mixed numbers.

**Does it collect student data?**  
The current single-file application has no account, analytics, advertising, or database. The interactive math runs in the browser. If you visit the hosted website, GitHub Pages and your browser may still process ordinary web requests.
